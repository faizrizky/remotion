# Story Series Reference Teardown (Channel 2, NOT finance)

Two reference Shorts studied frame by frame (720x1280, 30 fps):
- **REF-A "horror/rules"** (98.8 s): 3D mannequin, night subway, "never board an empty train" rules, fork = Get on / Stay.
- **REF-B "consequence sim"** (87.0 s): 2D inked cartoon + info cards + game HUD, fork = HIDE / SPEND, "majority writes Part 2".

Decision (11 Nov): new channel is a different brand and a different topic (not finance). Visual direction = REF-B style (2D). The FORMAT below is topic-agnostic; only the "mechanic taught" changes with the topic.

This is a structural teardown for our own original stories. We copy the machine (structure, pacing, visual system), never their story, character, names, props or lines.

---

## 1. The one-sentence formula

> Second person, present tense. A situation you cannot undo -> a stack of hidden rules that each make it worse -> a "clever" plan -> a twist that the plan has a name / a trap -> a status recap -> a one-word fork the audience votes on in the comments -> "Part 2 tomorrow."

REF-A runs it with horror rules (a rule sheet is planted, then both rules trigger at once). REF-B runs it with real-world mechanics (each "problem" teaches one true fact, then the plan fails on a rule the viewer did not know).

Why it works: the viewer is the protagonist (no name), every beat reveals a rule the viewer did not know, and the ending is a decision, not a cliffhanger about "what happens", so the comment section writes the next episode.

## 2. Narration teardown (voice and script)

**Person / tense**: "You ..." present tense, always. No narrator-opinion, no backstory.

**Hook (0-7 s)**: three short sentences: (1) the irreversible act or situation, (2) the concrete physical detail (where / how many), (3) a challenge line: "And the hard part? It starts now." REF-A: "Imagine you miss the last train..." then "Alone."

**Sentence rhythm**
- 3 to 7 words per caption chunk, one idea per sentence. Fragments for tension ("Alone." "Breathing." "Right behind.").
- ~175-200 words in 87-98 s (about 115-125 wpm). Slow on purpose; the beat after a rule/number is silent or card-only.
- Counted enumerations are spoken as short beats: "Nine. Eighteen. Twenty-seven. Thirty-six thousand." (REF-B). Lists of numbers are never read in one breath.

**Devices (reuse these)**
1. **Numbered problems**: "Problem one. ... Problem two. ..." each ends with the problem unresolved, so the next one stacks.
2. **"Here's what you don't know."** The pivot sentence right before the hidden mechanic is revealed (REF-B ~0:42).
3. **False relief then reversal**: "You get smart." (plan) -> "Except that pattern has a name." (REF-B), "You smile and think: just a joke." -> lights go out (REF-A).
4. **Rule planted, rule triggered**: a written rule is shown early; later the scene breaks it for the character (REF-A, 0:29 -> 1:06). In REF-B the rule card is "Never spend it near where you took it."
5. **Risk meter language**: a number that rises (suspicion 3% -> 35%) so tension is measurable.
6. **Dry one-liner before the fork**: "ten million dollars and you're sick of motel noodles." A small laugh right before the dilemma.
7. **Fork in one word**: "You can hide it. Or you can... COMMENT ONE WORD: HIDE / SPEND." Both words are verbs, both sound tempting, neither is safe.
8. **Series promise**: "Part 2 tomorrow. The majority writes Part 2."

**Sentence templates (fill with our topic)**
- Hook: "You just [irreversible act]. It's [concrete detail]. And the hard part? It starts now."
- Problem: "Problem one. [Physical/legal/biological reality that blocks you]."
- Pivot: "Here's what you don't know."
- Plan: "So you get smart. [Plan]. [Neat rhythm line]."
- Twist: "Except [the plan] has a name."
- Escalation: "And it's exactly what [authority/nature/system] is [trained/built] to spot."
- Recap: "[Day N]. Where you stand."
- Fork: "You can [A]. Or you can [B]. Comment one word."

**What we must NOT copy**: their exact scenario, tagline ("Every dollar leaves a trail"), rule texts, names (Ashford Road), phrases like "What would you do?" used verbatim as the only closer. Use our own world and our own tagline per series.

## 4. Visual teardown (REF-B system; spec for our Remotion build)

**Canvas**: 1080x1920 (they are 720x1280; keep the same relative positions). 24 fps for our pipeline (they use 30). Hold the same cut rhythm in seconds.

**Two scene families, alternating**
1. **Story shots**: rich inked 2D illustration (comic line work, painted texture), dark moody lighting with one coloured neon source (magenta blinds in the motel), warm lamp, night car interior with city bokeh, daylight street/bank in blue-cream. Recurring side characters with stronger designs than the lead (teller with red glasses, investigator with moustache).
2. **Info cards**: flat designs, no illustration, big type, one mechanic per card.

**Lead character**: deliberately simple (white round head, dot eyes, thick eyebrows, small mouth, navy cap, grey hoodie, black stick legs, white sneakers). Expressiveness through a small expression set: neutral, squint/strain, sweat drop (stress), worried, shocked. The lead is generic on purpose so the viewer is "you".

**Palette (sampled)**
| Token | Hex | Use |
|-------|-----|-----|
| cream | #F0EADE | info card ground (with faint paper grain) |
| ink-dark | #15100D | dark card ground |
| accent pink | #E84888 | emphasised word, stamp, risk bar, active HUD value |
| money green | ~#8FB27A with dark outline | icon fills |
| text on cream | #15100D | |
| text on dark | #F5F0E6 | |

**Type**
- Big card type and captions: heavy geometric sans (Inter/SF-style, weight 700-800), tight tracking.
- Small labels: monospace caps (e.g. "PROBLEM 1 - THE WEIGHT", "THAT PATTERN HAS A NAME", "DAY 3 - WHERE YOU STAND"), 10-12 px at 720 width, letter-spaced, low contrast.
- One pink-accented word per headline ("EVERY DOLLAR LEAVES A **TRAIL**", "A **TON**", "**Just** under the line").

**Captions (kinetic, burned-in)**
- Sentence case, bold white, centred, at about 73% of frame height (about y 1400 of 1920).
- **Karaoke reveal**: the whole phrase is on screen, words not yet spoken are grey (about 45% opacity), each word turns white when spoken.
- 2-4 words per chunk; changes about every 0.7-1 s; sits over the picture with a soft dark shadow, never a box.
- Caption position moves up/down only to avoid faces and key numbers.
- Not present during card-only segments.

**HUD (story shots only)**
- Dark translucent rounded bar at top-left, about 14-15% from the top, three pills: CASH, BANKS, SUSPICION (monospace label above, bold value below).
- Values change at story moments (cash 10,000,000 -> 9,973,000; banks 0 -> 27,000; suspicion value turns pink when above 0).
- Under the HUD a small pink chip names the current beat ("PROBLEM 1", "RULE 1").
- Topic change: rename the three pills to the new topic's meters (see section 7).

**Info card animations (exact behaviour observed)**
- *Counter + grid card (0:12.5-0:20, about 7.5 s)*: mono label top -> one icon fades in -> caption "1 bill = 1 gram" with the pink "1 gram" -> icon grid fills row by row while a rolling-digit counter (slot-machine blur on fast digits) counts 0 -> 100,000 -> big result word pops ("100 kg") -> hold about 2 s.
- *Term + stamp card (0:59-1:05, about 5.5 s)*: label -> huge pink term -> one-line definition -> slightly rotated outlined stamp "FEDERAL CRIME" (about -3 degrees) -> tiny citation line -> risk bar with % counter 1 -> 35 filling in step.
- *Status card (1:12-1:17)*: dark "DAY 3" title for about 1 s -> cream card: three rows with labels and big numbers rolling simultaneously from the start state to the final state (digit blur), the risk bar filling, "Spent so far: $0" footer -> hold about 2 s.
- *Big-type card ("A TON")*: two words, one pink, mono equation under it, instant cut in.
- *Rule/end cards (dark)*: centred text only, mono label above, pink emphasis, nothing else.

**Camera inside story shots**: slow pans/tilts and push-ins (e.g. tilt down across the bed to reveal bags), starting on a detail then revealing. Shot types in rotation: extreme close-up on object, medium on character, over-the-shoulder, establishing exterior, POV (hands on the steering wheel), final hold on the face or window reflection.

**Transitions**: hard cuts (no fades, no wipes). Card in/out are instant; the energy comes from content, not transitions. About 30-35 cuts per 90 s.

**Annotation overlays on the picture**: a pink ring drawn around the key object, a small pink label tag with a thin leader (e.g. "BAIT MONEY / SERIAL NUMBER ON FILE"), a white rounded price tag popped over a character ("$27,000").

**REF-A visual differences** (kept only as inspiration): 3D mannequin render, cold blue-black palette after the lights go out, rule sheet as a prop with a red highlight box on rule 1, overlay chips "1 GET ON / 2 STAY" with leader lines to the two choices.

## 7. Topic transfer (non-finance): what changes and what stays

Stays: structure, narration devices, visual system, HUD, fork, series promise.
Changes: the "mechanic" taught on each card and the three HUD meters.

| Topic line | HUD meters | Mechanic cards teach | Fork words |
|-----------|-----------|----------------------|------------|
| Space survival ("You are the last crew member on a station") | OXYGEN / POWER / SANITY | real life-support limits, delay in radio, CO2 scrubbing | VENT / SEAL |
| Wilderness survival ("Lost in the mountains, phone at 4%") | BODY TEMP / WATER / HOPE | hypothermia timeline, water rule of 3, signalling | STAY / MOVE |
| Time travel ("You wake up in a past century") | BLEND-IN / FOOD / SUSPICION | period-accurate dangers and customs | RUN / HIDE |
| Rules horror (REF-A style in 2D) | SANITY / TIME / DISTANCE | the planted rules | ENTER / WAIT |
| Law/consequence ("You found/broke something") | RISK / LUCK / TIME | what the system actually does | KEEP / REPORT |
| Spy / identity ("You were recruited by mistake") | COVER / TRUST / TIME | tradecraft basics | TRUST / TEST |

Selection filter: each part must contain at least 2 true, checkable mechanics (cards), 1 numeric meter that moves, 1 binary fork with two attractive verbs.

## 8. Series engine

- **Series bible**: lead (generic), world, 5-7 rules or mechanics in order of discovery, the final answer (hidden), 6 planned twists, and an end state for every fork branch.
- **Each part**: one new hidden mechanic, one escalation of the meter, one fork. Part N+1 starts with the winning comment word and resolves it in the first 10 s, then escalates.
- **Cross-platform**: same 85-95 s vertical for Shorts / Reels / TikTok, comment CTA identical. YouTube long = 8-10 parts compiled with a recap card at each seam and a new intro.
- **Cadence**: daily or every other day keeps the "tomorrow" promise credible; publish only what is already produced.
- **Integrity**: tag "fiction / educational" in the description; mechanics must be factually checkable and sourced in the pinned comment; do not present fiction as a true story.
