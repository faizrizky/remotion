# RULE ZERO - Channel concept, identity, Series 1 bible, Part 1 script

Status: v1 (11 Nov 2026). Channel name decided: **Rule Zero**. Market: US. Language: English. Genre: rules-horror, second person, serialized, 2D inked animation. Format reference: see STORY_SERIES_REFERENCE_TEARDOWN.md (structure and visual system are reused; every story element below is original).

Premise default chosen by Claude (not yet confirmed by MattJr): the 24-hour laundromat. Swappable, see section 9.

---

## 1. Concept in one paragraph

Every episode puts "you" in a closed place overnight with a posted sheet of rules. The rules look like workplace trivia; each one gets triggered, and you must choose between obeying and the thing that is begging you to break it. Each part ends on a two-word fork the audience votes on in the comments; the winning word opens the next part. The channel name is the series mystery: every sheet is missing its first rule. **Rule Zero** is the rule nobody wrote down, and each series ends by revealing what it was.

Why the name works: short, sayable, curious, fits any location (laundromat now, motel, elevator, bus later), and pays off as the final twist of each series.

- **Name**: Rule Zero. Handle to claim (availability NOT verified from here): @rulezero, fallbacks @rulezerostories, @rulezero.tv, @wherezerorule (check each platform on a laptop; use the same handle on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram).
- **Tagline options** (pick one): "Read the sheet. Then choose." / "Every rule was written by someone who broke the last one." / "The first rule isn't on the sheet."
- **Fork signature** (end card line): "Comment one word. The majority writes Part 2."
- **Tone**: tense, dry-witted, never gory, PG-13. Humor is one deadpan line per part, right before the fork.
- **Content boundaries**: fiction only and labelled as fiction; no real people, real tragedies, or "true story" claims; no gore, no harm to children; no instructions that could be copied for self-harm; nothing that could be mistaken for a real emergency. Advertiser-safe horror (suspense, not shock).
- **Distinct from Penny Logics**: different palette, font set, lead character, sting, and narrator voice. Do not reuse backgrounds or SFX sets without a deliberate decision.

## 3. The lead and the threat

**Lead ("the Attendant")**: deliberately generic so the viewer is "you". Round white head, dot eyes, thick brows, small mouth, tousled hair silhouette, mustard-yellow work vest over a grey tee, sleeves rolled, black stick limbs, white sneakers. **Blank name tag** ("HELLO MY NAME IS ____") on the vest: the viewer fills in the name. Expression set: neutral, squint, sweat drop, worry, shock, flat deadpan.

**The Regular (threat, Series 1)**: tall figure in a long damp coat carrying a laundry basket, never seen face-on; only reflected in dryer glass or as a silhouette behind the frosted door. When it appears, the lead's reflection is missing in the same glass. No gore, no jump-scare faces.

**Supporting**: the owner (kind-faced, tired, always "back at six"), later the first attendant (only as handwriting).

## 4. Series 1: "The Closing Shift" (setting: Spin Cycle Coin Laundry, Route 9, 24 hours)

**Constraint (timeline lock)**: shift 11:00 PM to 6:00 AM. The door has a timer lock that engages at 11:00 PM; the owner returns at 6:00 AM. HUD clock counts up through the night.

**HUD (story shots)**: three pills, top-left at about 14% from the top: **CLOCK** (11:00 PM ... 6:00 AM), **NERVE** (100% down to 0%), **DOORS** (LOCKED / UNLOCKED). A small yellow chip under it names the beat ("RULE 1", "PROBLEM 2").

### The posted sheet: "ATTENDANT RULES" (typed, laminated, taped to the coin machine)
(Rule numbers 1-7 visible; Rule 3 is scratched out with marker; no Rule 0.)

1. Dryer 7 is out of order. If it is running, do not open it.
2. Customers leave by 2:00 AM. Anyone still here at 2:00 is not a customer.
3. ██████████ (scratched out so hard the laminate is torn)
4. Do not return a lost item to anyone who describes it before they see it.
5. If the lights flicker twice, count the people inside. Do not count yourself.
6. The back door is staff only. It knocks three times. Never four.
7. Do not answer the pay phone.

**Hidden truth (series answer, do not reveal until Part 8)**
- The laundromat "keeps" something: whatever is left of people who do not make it to morning. Machine 7 is where it is stored.
- The sheet was written one rule at a time by every night attendant before you; each rule is the last mistake someone made. Rule 3 was scratched out by the first attendant, because it explains too much.
- **Rule Zero (never printed)**: "Someone has to be on shift." If nobody is on shift when the clock hits 11:00, the doors open and what is inside machine 7 walks out. The owner is the first attendant: he never left, and "back at six" is the only way he can be released for the day.
- Surviving to 6:00 AM is not the win condition. The win condition is deciding who closes next. Ending fork of the series: **LEAVE / STAY** (leave = whoever is hired next becomes Rule 8; stay = you become the new owner).

### Part plan (each about 90 s, vertical, fork in the last 12 s)
| Part | Title | Rule triggered | New mechanic revealed | HUD change | Fork (two verbs) |
|------|-------|----------------|-----------------------|-----------|------------------|
| 1 | Dryer 7 | R1 | The machine runs with no power; voice inside is yours | NERVE 100 -> 71 | OPEN / WAIT |
| 2 | Two A.M. | R2 | Two customers who left are back, seated, folded clothes not theirs | NERVE 71 -> 55 | SPEAK / IGNORE |
| 3 | Lost and Found | R4 | A woman describes an item that has not been lost yet | NERVE -> 48 | RETURN / KEEP |
| 4 | The Count | R5 | Lights flicker twice; you count 3 people and you see 2 | NERVE -> 37 | COUNT / LEAVE |
| 5 | Four Knocks | R6 | The fourth knock comes in the owner's rhythm | DOORS flickers | OPEN / HOLD |
| 6 | Pay Phone | R7 | It rings with the lobby's number; the caller is the owner | NERVE -> 22 | ANSWER / UNPLUG |
| 7 | Rule Three | R3 | You find the torn text behind the coin box | CLOCK 5:12 AM | READ / BURN |
| 8 | Back at Six | R0 | The owner arrives. Rule Zero. Who closes next | DOORS UNLOCKED | LEAVE / STAY |

Each part: one rule triggered, one fact about the building, one NERVE drop, one fork. The winning word is the first 10 s of the next part. The long YouTube video = Parts 1-8 compiled (about 13-15 min) with a recap card at each seam and a fresh intro.

## 5. Part 1 - "Dryer 7": script, timings, shots

Target 90 s. About 184 spoken words (about 2.2 words/s average, about 2.7 words/s in the hook, matching the reference pacing). Fork in the last 12 s. Every card animation must finish before the next cut.

### Script (spoken, English)
**0:00** You just took the night shift at a laundromat. The doors lock at eleven. The owner's back at six.

**0:07** *[Title card: RULE ZERO / SERIES 1: THE CLOSING SHIFT / PART 1: DRYER 7]*

**0:10** Problem one. You can't leave. The lock runs on a timer, and the timer isn't yours.
**0:17** Before he left, he taped one sheet to the coin machine.
**0:22** Rule one: dryer seven is out of order. If it's running, don't open it.
**0:28** You almost smile. You check. It's unplugged.
**0:32** Rule two: customers leave by two a.m. Anyone still here at two isn't a customer.
**0:39** Rule three? Scratched out. Marker pressed so hard it tore the laminate.
**0:45** Problem two. It's 11:47. Dryer seven starts to turn.
**0:50** No cord. No power. Warm air rolling out of the vent.
**0:55** Then you hear it. Knocking. From inside the drum.
**0:59** Slow. Patient. Three knocks.
**1:03** And a voice. Soft. Familiar. "Please. It's cold in here."
**1:08** It's your voice.
**1:11** You've been here forty minutes. Your first review is going great.
**1:16** Rule one says don't open it. The drum says please.
**1:20** You can open it. Or you can wait six hours and eleven minutes. *(on-screen: COMMENT ONE WORD: OPEN / WAIT)*
**1:26** *(end card)* Part 2 tomorrow. The majority writes Part 2.

## 6. Production pipeline (new channel)
1. Lock concept (this doc) -> MattJr confirms premise and tagline.
2. Character + threat design in Gemini (reference sheet), approved before any shot.
3. Shot illustrations (list above) -> upscale if soft.
4. Voice: record Part 1 narration (voice distinct from Penny Logics), then word-timing for karaoke.
5. Remotion: build `RuleSheetCard`, `StatHud`, `KaraokeCaption`, `ForkCard`, `TypeCard`, camera moves, then assemble Part 1.
6. QA against the teardown checklist (cut rhythm, card finish before transition, no overlap, caption contrast, HUD readable on every shot).
7. Publish pack (title formula, description tagged fiction, pinned comment with the fork).

Title formula for this channel (adapts rule 55 of the Penny Logics rules): "[Number/rule] + curiosity + the object", e.g. "Dryer 7 Is Out of Order. It's Running. (Part 1)". Keep under about 55 characters, no ALL CAPS.

## 7. Success metrics and kill criteria (first 8 parts)
- Retention to 80% watch-through on Shorts, comment rate (fork words per view), and share of viewers returning for Part N+1.
- If after 4 parts comments per 1,000 views stay below 5 or the fork words are not appearing, change the fork wording and the hook, not the format.

## 8. Risks to manage
- Resemblance to the reference (subway rules story): keep every element original; avoid "never board", "no passengers", "footsteps", and the "What would you do?" closer.
- Horror content and the "inauthentic content" policy: unique story, voice, and visuals per episode; label fiction.
