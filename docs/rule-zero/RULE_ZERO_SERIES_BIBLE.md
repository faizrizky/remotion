# RULE ZERO - Channel concept, identity, Series 1 bible, Part 1 script

Status: v2 (11 Nov 2026). Series 1 premise confirmed by MattJr: **CCTV operator in a multi-level parking garage; on Level 3 one car always moves first.** (v1 laundromat premise is parked as a Series 2 candidate, see section 4.) Channel name: **Rule Zero**. Market: US. Language: English. Genre: rules-horror, second person, serialized, 2D inked animation. Format reference: STORY_SERIES_REFERENCE_TEARDOWN.md (structure and visual system are reused; every story element below is original).

---

## 1. Concept in one paragraph

Every series puts "you" in a closed place overnight with a posted sheet of rules. The rules look like workplace trivia; each one gets triggered, and you must choose between obeying and the thing begging you to break it. Each part ends on a one-word fork the audience votes on in the comments; the winning word opens the next part. The channel name is the series mystery: every sheet is missing its first rule. **Rule Zero** is the rule nobody wrote down, and each series ends by revealing it.

Series 1 hook in one line: *you only ever see the garage through cameras, and on Level 3 one car always moves before everything else does.*

Why the premise works: the viewer watches the same monitor wall as the lead, so every scare is a "spot the difference" the audience can play along with (which car moved? which bay?). Nothing needs a monster on screen: an empty driver's seat is enough. Cheap to produce too: CCTV-look shots are a Remotion treatment over one clean garage illustration.

## 3. The lead and the threat

**Lead ("the Operator")**: deliberately generic so the viewer is "you". Round white head, dot eyes, thick brows, small mouth, tousled hair silhouette, headset with a signal-yellow band, grey hoodie, lanyard with a **blank badge** ("OPERATOR: ____", the viewer fills in the name), black stick limbs. Expressions: neutral, squint, sweat drop, worry, shock, flat deadpan.

**Car 31 (the Sedan)**: a plain grey boxy sedan, no brand marks, no plate text (plate is a blank rectangle; any text is added in Remotion). Always parked slightly crooked. Headlights off until it wants to be seen. Never has a visible driver; the driver's seat is empty or too dark to read.

**The Driver** (revised 12 Nov, to match the reference's on-screen threat): the visible scare of the series. A tall, thin black silhouette, blank round head, no face except two small amber dots (the Car 31 headlight motif; not white eyes, not a twin of the Operator). Always at the edge of the frame, always seen through glass or a screen (stairwell door glass, monitor reflection, windshield), never in the open, never face-on, never touches anyone, no gore (PG-13). The lead never looks at it directly in Part 1; the viewer sees it first. Appears only in the last 15 s of each part, a little closer each time.

**Supporting**: Dale, the night guard (walks rounds, never reads the sheet, friendly), and the previous operator (only as handwriting and her log entries).

## 4. Series 1: "Level 3" (setting: Harbor Street Parking Structure, 6 levels, open 24 hours)

**Constraint (timeline lock)**: shift 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM. You are alone in the control booth with 8 monitors; the only way up is the stairs or the elevator, and the sheet says you do not go up. Dale walks a round on the hour.

**HUD (story shots)**: three pills, top-left at about 14% from the top: **CLOCK** (10:00 PM ... 6:00 AM), **NERVE** (100% down to 0%), **LOG** (entry count, 0 ... 8). A yellow chip under it names the beat ("PROBLEM 1", "RULE 1"). CCTV frames also carry a REC dot, camera label ("CAM 3-A") and a timestamp, composed in Remotion.

### The posted sheet: "OPERATOR RULES" (typed, laminated, taped to the monitor bezel)
(Rules 1-7 visible; Rule 3 is scratched out with pen pressed through the paper; no Rule 0.)

1. At the top of every hour, check Level 3. One car will have moved. Log it. Do not touch it.
2. Never send security to Level 3.
3. ██████████ (scratched out so hard the paper is torn)
4. Camera 3-C has no recording. If it shows you, look away.
5. If the elevator calls itself, do not watch it arrive.
6. If a car's headlights are on in an empty bay, do not use the speaker.
7. Do not answer the intercom on Level 3.

**Hidden truth (series answer, reveal only in Part 8)**
- Level 3 is where the garage keeps what it has "parked": the cars on it are the ones whose drivers never came back. The one that moves first is the scout: it shows the bay that will be needed next.
- The sheet was written one rule at a time by every operator before you; each rule is the last mistake someone made. Rule 3 was scratched out by the first operator because it explains too much.
- **Rule Zero (never printed)**: "The log must never be empty." The log is the only thing that holds Level 3 in place. Every logged move is a promise that the car stays where it was put. If a shift ends with no operator and no entry, Level 3 comes down.
- Surviving to 6:00 AM is not the win condition. The win condition is deciding who keeps the log. Ending fork: **CLOCK OUT / STAY** (clock out = the next hire becomes Rule 8; stay = you become the new first operator).

## 5. Part 1 - "Space 31": script, timings, shots

Target 90 s, about 190 spoken words (about 2.2 words/s average, about 2.4 in the hook). Fork in the last 12 s. Every card animation must finish before the next cut. Re-check the word count and timing against the actual voice take.

### Storytelling style lock (mirrors the reference; applies to every part)
1. **Second person, present tense, no backstory.** The viewer is "you". Fragments carry the fear: "Alone." "No driver." "Right behind."
2. **Hook in three beats**: the act you cannot undo, the concrete detail, the challenge. Then one lonely fragment.
3. **Rule planted, then triggered.** The sheet is shown early (0:22-0:44). Both rules break at the same moment near the end (R1 car moved + R2 Dale goes up).
4. **False relief, then reversal.** "You almost smile. Cars don't move." -> the sedan moves.
5. **The threat is IN the scene, close behind the lead, in the last 15 s** (The Driver in the glass). Slow push-in, no jump scare, no music hit until the fork.
6. **Fork as in-scene chips** with leader lines pointing at the two real objects (radio / monitor), not a separate dark card. Final frame = the lead's shocked face with the threat still faintly present.
7. **One dry line before the fork** ("First night. Great start.") so the dread gets a small laugh.
8. Slow pace (about 115-125 wpm), calm voice, silent beat after every rule. Never raise the voice.
We copy the machine, not the lines: no "Imagine...", no white-eyed twin, no "What would you do?" as the closer.

### Script (spoken, English)
**0:00** You just took the night shift at a parking garage. Alone. Eight screens. One rule: watch Level three.

**0:07** *[Title card: RULE ZERO / SERIES 1: LEVEL 3 / PART 1: SPACE 31]*

**0:10** Problem one. The only other person here is a guard who walks the lot by himself.
**0:16** Before she quit, the last operator taped a sheet to the monitor.
**0:21** Rule one: at the top of every hour, check Level three. One car will have moved. Log it. Don't touch it.
**0:29** You almost smile. Cars don't move.
**0:32** Rule two: never send security to Level three.
**0:37** Rule three? Scratched out. Pen pressed through the paper.
**0:43** Problem two. Eleven o'clock. You check Level three.
**0:47** A grey sedan. Bay thirty-one.
**0:50** You rewind. An hour ago, bay twelve. No brake lights. No driver.
**0:57** You log it. Rule one, followed.
**1:00** Then the headlights come on. Slowly. Aimed at the camera.
**1:05** On the other screen, Dale scans in at Level three. Rule two, broken. He doesn't know. Nobody told him.
**1:12** Past the open door, something tall is standing.
**1:17** Dale doesn't lift his flashlight.
**1:20** First night. Great start.
**1:22** You can warn him. Or you can watch what it does next. *(on-screen chips: 1 WARN / 2 WATCH)*
**1:30** *(final frame + end card)* Comment one, warn. Or two, watch. Part 2 tomorrow. The majority writes Part 2.

## 6. Production pipeline
1. Lock concept (this doc) -> MattJr confirms tagline and remaining decisions.
2. Operator + Car 31 design in Gemini (reference sheet), approved before any shot.
3. Shot illustrations (list above) -> upscale if soft.
4. Voice: record Part 1 narration (voice distinct from Penny Logics), then word timing for karaoke.
5. Remotion: build `RuleSheetCard`, `LogBookCard`, `CctvFrame`, `StatHud`, `KaraokeCaption`, `ForkCard`, `TypeCard`, camera moves, then assemble Part 1.
6. QA against the teardown checklist.
7. Publish pack (title formula, description tagged fiction, pinned comment with the fork).

Title formula: "[rule/number] + curiosity + the object", e.g. "The Car on Level 3 Always Moves First (Part 1)". Under about 55 characters, no ALL CAPS.

## 7. Success metrics and kill criteria (first 8 parts)
- Retention to 80% watch-through on Shorts, comment rate (fork words per view), share of viewers returning for Part N+1.
- If after 4 parts comments per 1,000 views stay below 5 or fork words are not appearing, change the fork wording and the hook, not the format.

## 8. Risks to manage
- Resemblance to the reference (subway rules story): keep every element original; avoid "never board", "no passengers", "footsteps", and the "What would you do?" closer.
- "Monitor wall + watched thing moves" is a known creepypasta shape (security-guard stories): keep our specifics (log, bay numbers, Rule Zero "the log must never be empty") front and centre.
- Horror content and the "inauthentic content" policy: unique story, voice, visuals per episode; label fiction.
- Cadence promise ("tomorrow"): publish only what is already produced.
